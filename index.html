Portfolio overview

Infrastructure work under real production constraints.

This portfolio presents selected work in Linux infrastructure, edge systems, and long-term operational reliability. The projects reflect production constraints rather than idealized greenfield environments: mixed hardware generations, long-lived operating-system images, peripheral change, fragmented knowledge, and the need to introduce improvements without interrupting continuous manufacturing operations.

The principal case study documents primary operational ownership of a 150+ device Raspberry Pi fleet supporting MES access, label printing, production displays, operational dashboards, and warehouse terminals across multiple facilities.

Engineering approach

Practical systems administration with durable outcomes.

Linux-first operations

Practical use of Ansible, system imaging, staged pilots, rollback baselines, and direct hardware validation.

Controlled complexity

Reduction of unnecessary software, services, and operational dependencies where they do not support the required function.

Rebuild-based migration

Rebuild-based migration when in-place changes would preserve unreliable assumptions or unsupported platform states.

Clear technical boundaries

Clear separation between implemented behavior, proposed architecture, and future validation.

Operational documentation

Documentation treated as an operational deliverable and reviewed with the operators, team leaders, and support staff expected to use it.

Human-owned automation

Closing note

Infrastructure should remain understandable after change.

The enduring value of infrastructure work is not simply that a system runs today. It is that the system can be understood, repaired, migrated, and operated safely as platforms, personnel, and operational requirements change.

The work presented here emphasizes that kind of ownership.
